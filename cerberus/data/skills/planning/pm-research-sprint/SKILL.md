## When to Use

Before committing engineering time to a new product idea, feature, or pivot. Use when the question is *"should we build this?"* not *"how do we build this?"*. Sized for a solo technical founder doing the research themselves — about 90 minutes of focused work end-to-end. Cherry-picked from github.com/phuryn/pm-skills.

## Procedure

Run the five steps in order. Output a one-page brief that covers all five.

### STEP 1 — Problem Definition (15 min)

Answer in **one sentence**:

> "Who has this problem, when do they have it, and what do they do today instead?"

If you cannot answer this in one sentence, the problem is not defined yet. Stop and refine before continuing.

### STEP 2 — Competitive Landscape (30 min)

For each competitor (5–10 of them):

- What do they do well?
- What do users complain about? (Check App Store reviews, Reddit, G2 / Capterra, "Ask HN: alternatives to X" threads.)

Produce a table:

| Competitor | Strength | Weakness | Price | Market position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| … | … | … | … | … |

### STEP 3 — Opportunity Sizing (20 min)

- **TAM** — total addressable market. A rough order of magnitude is enough; do not over-estimate precision.
- **Your wedge** — the specific segment you can realistically reach *first* with your unfair advantage.
- **Why now** — what's changed that makes this viable today and wasn't last year?

### STEP 4 — Risk Matrix (15 min)

List the top **5 assumptions** your plan depends on. For each:

- **Confidence it's true** (1–5)
- **Impact if wrong** (1–5)
- **A cheap test** — how could you falsify it for less than a day's work?

Sort the rows by `confidence × impact` and tackle the scariest assumption first.

### STEP 5 — Go / No-Go Criteria (10 min)

Define **3 conditions** that would make you kill this idea. Write them down before you start building. If you cannot define them, you have confirmation bias — the assumptions in Step 4 weren't honest.

**Rules**
- Time-box the steps. Going over 90 minutes total means you are building, not researching.
- Every claim about competitors must cite a source — reviews, threads, pricing pages. Cited rivalry is research; uncited rivalry is vibes.
- The Go / No-Go conditions must be *measurable*. "It doesn't feel right" is not a kill criterion.
